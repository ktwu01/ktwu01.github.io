I just turned 23. If I had to sum up the past year in one sentence, I would say: skin in the game, with authority, responsibility, and benefits aligned.

> Author: [Koutian Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ktwu01/); [GitHub: ktwu01](https://github.com/ktwu01/)

## I have had days when I only thought and did nothing

In 2024, I spent a period focusing too much on methodology without taking much action. That experience brought me a major failure and a major existential crisis. When I was not doing anything, I felt anxious and panicked. I knew I should do something, but the more anxious I became, the harder it was to get out of that state.

For a long time afterward, I simply stopped bothering with structure and started doing things. Spontaneously, however I felt like it, doing whatever came to mind. At least once I had done something, I had something to face and results to look at.

But now I have started thinking again: if I just keep doing things, will I know what I want? Whose wishes am I fulfilling with all the things keeping me busy?

## I am starting to see my relationships with others more clearly

For a long time, I was trying to become an employee. Looking back through my old job-search notes, I see myself repeatedly working out how to match a role, prepare a resume, and convince others that I could get things done. Back then, I was used to first thinking about how to finish what others assigned me, while my own boundaries were not clear enough.

But in those notes, I also wrote that ultimately, I was working toward my own goals. I wanted to improve through work and gain the ability to do what I wanted to do. Rereading them now, I realize that I had already asked myself this question long ago; I still needed the experiences that followed to keep understanding it.

Recently, I have started to realize that some people are draining me. Some relationships are helping me. I am meeting more different people and developing closer connections with some of them, especially since starting a startup.

In the past, I tended to focus on whether something got done. Now I am starting to notice why I agree to do something and how I feel within a relationship. There is a big difference between someone who can always get me to do things and someone who can help me grow.

Now I am becoming clearer about what I want, what I can accept, and why. I am starting to set boundaries for myself and understand what those boundaries mean to me. This is how I now understand personal agency.

For any particular undertaking, the responsibilities and risks I take on should match my decision-making authority and benefits. I want to use this standard to reconsider my place in work and collaboration.

## My cofounders have helped me see my own problems

One clear change since starting a startup is that people correct me directly.

Some friends used to leave my views unchallenged. Now my cofounders point out the things they think I have wrong. Sometimes it feels as though they bring up almost every problem they see.

These exchanges are like a mirror. Through other people's feedback, I can better understand myself and the world. I need to understand why they disagree and where my own judgments come from.

Starting a company has made these interactions closer. We have to work together. Our judgments affect our shared work, and we also have to face their consequences. I need to go back and think about the problems others point out; I also need to put my ideas up for discussion to find out whether they hold up.

This has changed how I understand reflection. After experiencing the failure of thinking without acting, I now feel again that reflection is helping me understand what I am going through.

## I am also learning to think from the other person's perspective

Another thing I am learning now is to think altruistically and find ways for both sides to win.

Once I am clear about my own goals and boundaries, I also need to understand what the other person wants. What does this collaboration mean to them? What do they need to take on, and what can they get from it? I want my own authority, responsibility, and benefits to align, and the other person deserves the same treatment.

I want to learn to consider both sides' needs together and find ways of working that both sides are willing to pursue and can benefit from.

## Before thinking about AI and startups, I need to understand myself

I want to use this essay to clarify my views on AI and startups. As I write, I find it hard to separate these questions from myself.

I am a person first, then a researcher and an entrepreneur. How I understand failure, respond to other people's opinions, and decide why to agree to something will all affect how I do research and lead others in technical work.

Writing is also helping me understand myself. Some thoughts are only feelings in my head; writing them down lets me see what I am actually saying. When I write that "some people are draining me," I also keep thinking about which of their behaviors bother me and why I keep agreeing.

I am reading and writing. I learn about the world by doing things and about myself through my relationships with others. Writing down these experiences also helps me gradually see my own story more clearly.
