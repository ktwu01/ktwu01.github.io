I recently met a senior lady, Ms. C, who has one habit wherever she goes. Before anything else, she asks where exactly the pain point is.

> Author: [Koutian Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ktwu01/); [GitHub: ktwu01](https://github.com/ktwu01/)

Ms. C studied history when she was young, and she had her heart set on revolution. Then one day she saw a new word in the newspaper, semiconductor. The paper called it a technology revolution. She was thrilled. She had missed the revolution in her own field, and she was not going to miss this one.

So she became an intern at a company, working on something named the bar code. The bar code was literally a standard, one shared way to tag a product, so a single scan could pull up its information.

Most interns would start by learning the technology. Not her. She went around asking everyone the same question. Exactly what is the pain point. What is the pain point of semiconductors.

The answer she got was the shop floor. A factory floor is a complicated place, different people coming and going, different machines stacked together, and figuring out how to run the whole mess was the real problem.

Picture the computers of that era. A single computer took up several rooms, tall as a wall. Workers who needed to move a program around had to walk a long way and spend a long time doing it. The machine just stood there, and the people serving it wore themselves out first.

Honestly, what stayed with me is simple. Half a century later, machines have shrunk from several rooms into a screen in your pocket, and her way of working has not aged a day. Do not rush to show off the technology. Ask first where the pain point is.
