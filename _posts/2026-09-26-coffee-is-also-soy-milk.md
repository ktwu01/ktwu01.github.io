Coffee is soy milk. Milk is soy milk too.

> Author: [Koutian Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ktwu01/); [GitHub: ktwu01](https://github.com/ktwu01/)

One morning I held a coffee in one hand and a croissant in the other, took a bite, and scattered flakes all over the table. My first thought: this is just soy milk and youtiao.

Youtiao is a long fried dough stick, the standard Chinese breakfast partner for warm soy milk. You tear it and dip it. Swap the cup, swap the bread, and the motion is identical. A hot drink, a crisp piece of carbohydrate. Dip, bite, sip.

## Different places, same pattern

Once you start counting, it shows up everywhere:

- China: soy milk with youtiao, torn and dipped.
- France: coffee with a croissant, often dunked straight into the bowl.
- Italy: cappuccino with a cornetto, the Italian cousin of the croissant.
- Spain: hot chocolate with churros, a long fried pastry made for dipping.
- Britain: tea with biscuits, where dunking is close to a national debate.
- India: chai, sweet milky tea (often spiced, as masala chai), with rusk, a twice-baked dry toast meant to be soaked.
- Turkey: tea with simit, a sesame-covered bread ring.
- Hong Kong: milk tea with a pineapple bun.
- Singapore and Malaysia: kopi, the local coffee, with kaya toast.
- Mongolia and Xinjiang: milk tea with fried dough, toasted millet, or naan.

So coffee is soy milk. So are milk, black tea, milk tea, and hot chocolate. A hot liquid plus a crisp carbohydrate seems to have been the default across cultures for thousands of years.

## Why this pattern

A few guesses. I may not have all of them right.

**In the morning you need water and fuel.** After a night of sleep you are short on water and on fuel. A hot drink rehydrates and warms you. Carbohydrate turns into energy fastest. One cup and one piece handle both jobs.

**It is cheap and it keeps.** Fried or dry-baked dough holds little water, so it spoils slowly and can be made ahead. Beans, tea leaves, and coffee beans store well too. For an ordinary person's breakfast, those two facts matter more than presentation.

**Hard things need softening.** This may be the oldest reason. Old bread went stale and hard; soaking it in something hot made it edible again. According to etymology dictionaries, the English word "soup" is related to "sop," and their shared ancestor, suppa, meant bread soaked in broth. A word does not prove where a dish came from, but for a long time "soup" often meant the whole bowl, bread and broth together. The Chinese dish yangrou paomo, flatbread torn into mutton broth, follows the same idea.

**The contrast is addictive.** The dipped end has soaked up the liquid while the rest is still crisp, so one bite gives you both. That contrast is the pleasure. In 1998 Len Fisher, a physicist at the University of Bristol, calculated how long to dunk a biscuit before it breaks, work that won the 1999 Ig Nobel Prize in Physics. When a physicist takes a small habit seriously enough to model it, people clearly care.

## What "thousands of years" means

When I say thousands of years, I need to be precise: no single pairing is that old.

Coffee spread across the world only a few centuries ago. The croissant only became popular in France during the nineteenth century. Accounts of when soy milk and youtiao became a common breakfast disagree, and I am not sure.

What is old is the structure, not any specific pair. Bread soaked in broth, wine, or milk has a long history in many civilizations. The drinks and the pastries kept changing. The template, a hot liquid plus a dry carbohydrate, did not.

## What I take from it

This interests me because it reminds me that much of what we think belongs only to our own culture turns out, when you take it apart, to be a similar answer humans reached under similar conditions.

Mornings are cold, people are hungry, money and time are short. Put that anywhere, and someone will pick up a hot cup and something to dip in it.

Next time I eat breakfast abroad and see someone dunk something into their cup, I will probably laugh: oh, you have soy milk and youtiao too.
