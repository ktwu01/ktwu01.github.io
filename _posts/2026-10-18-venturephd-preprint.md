**Original Publication Date:** March 15, 2026

> Author: [Koutian Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ktwu01/); [GitHub: ktwu01](https://github.com/ktwu01/)

A PhD student can spend years on a project and an advisor before learning whether that relationship will train them or trap them. One person controls their salary, their lab access, their evaluation, and their recommendation letters. Leaving costs almost everything. Venture capital has handled this kind of lopsided bet for decades with contracts that bind both sides and price the exit. My new preprint, *VenturePhD*, imports that logic into doctoral education.


The paper is on [SocArXiv](https://osf.io/preprints/socarxiv/7hd3u_v1) and [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/23287504).

## The design

VenturePhD pairs reciprocal commitments with supported transfer and exit rights. It separates two decisions that the current system fuses:

- whether a project continues, and
- whether the student keeps funding and credit for the work already done.

Students and supervisors agree on research and training plans they can revise. The university guarantees specified funding, appoints reviewers outside the supervisory relationship, and pays for time-limited transitions when a student changes direction. The paper spells out who owes what, the remedies when someone defaults, a worked example, and hypotheses for a pilot.

## What it does not claim

It reports no intervention results. The open question is empirical: can a university make a student less dependent on one supervisor without hurting research quality, mentoring, or access to the PhD?

This builds on my March [research compendium on bilateral VC contracts and PhD programs](/posts/2026/03/research-grounding-bilateral-vc-phd-programs/), which collected the evidence. VenturePhD is the proposal.
