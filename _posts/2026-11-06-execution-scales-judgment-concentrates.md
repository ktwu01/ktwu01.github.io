A high school student can now submit to Science. The student did not pick the question.

> Author: [Koutian Wu](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ktwu01/); [GitHub: ktwu01](https://github.com/ktwu01/)

On May 27 I wrote that everything is becoming distributed. Companies, learning, collaboration, anything that involves people. Writing papers, all kinds of other work, and large-scale collaboration most of all. Even a PhD is turning into a distributed task: one paper can carry many authors, and one person's PhD can be supported by many people.

I drew a few consequences. In papers, it means undergrads publishing. The barrier to starting a company keeps dropping, so more and more people start one, and most of them die. The number of arbitrageurs won't necessarily grow, because human nature is what it is. But the ones who do it will keep getting better. Winner takes all.

Five months later, the evidence is in.

A professor at BU told me that yesterday he worked with a high school student on a paper submitted to Science, AI-related. If it gets in, it means that with AI, a high schooler can publish in Science. The barrier to doing research is already very low.

The same day, at the [SciFM conference](https://www.scifmconferences.org/), a CS professor said that compared with someone who cannot code, he now has no advantage at all.

Film tells the same story. Epics that used to need huge crews and huge money now need very few people. Latvia's [*Flow*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_(2024_film)) was made in the open-source tool Blender on a budget of about €3.5 million, with four or five people working at once in the studio. It beat Pixar for the Oscar for Best Animated Feature. China's [*Niu Lai*](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cul/2026/08-17/10678996.shtml), released this August, goes further: a director and his 60-year-old mother, two people, five years, roughly ¥100,000 in hard costs, and an 86-minute animated feature in theaters. Plenty of AI films don't need many people either.

So was I right in May? Half right.

"Distributed" was the wrong word. What is actually happening: as AI improves, execution becomes more and more scalable. One person plus AI is what a team used to be. Code, experiments, 3D models, edits, first drafts, everything that once took headcount can now be copied without limit.

Once execution scales, it stops being worth much. The value moves somewhere else. Judgment and attention are concentrating.

Look at the high schooler's Science paper. Who chose the question? Who decided which result was worth sending to Science, and which result was wrong? The professor. "High schoolers can publish in Science" really means a professor with good questions suddenly has unlimited hands.

Look at the CS professor. He says he has no edge over someone who cannot code. If his edge was only that he could code, he's right, and he's finished. If his edge is knowing which problems are worth solving, it just grew tenfold. His remark exposes how many people never worked out what they are actually paid for.

Look at the two films. Both teams were absurdly small. The outcomes could not be further apart. *Flow* won an Oscar. *Niu Lai* earned about ¥7,000 in its first ten days, then went viral because its 3D models were shockingly ugly, and [climbed to ¥170,000](https://www.sohu.com/a/1063251239_121814834). People bought tickets to laugh at it. Both teams managed the execution. The difference was taste and judgment. Whatever *Niu Lai* made later came entirely from attention, and attention gets scarcer the more films there are.

Seen this way, my May predictions form one causal chain.

More people start companies and more of them die because "can you build it" is no longer the filter. The filter moved downstream: does anyone want it, does anyone trust it, does anyone see it.

Arbitrageurs get stronger and winners take all because once trying costs almost nothing, the game is judging which attempt deserves to be scaled. Judgment compounds. Execution doesn't. Execution is tap water now.

When a PhD becomes a distributed task, the degree changes meaning too. It used to prove you could do research on your own. Now it proves you can direct a crowd of people and a pile of AI to get research right. If a high schooler can publish in Science, a Science paper starts losing value as a signal. Evaluation will shift to what is harder to fake: a long track record, and who is willing to put their own name behind you.

For an individual, the conclusion is simple. Stop competing on execution. AI has already paved that road. Own a good question, or own someone's trust, or own a channel where you get seen. Without any of the three, the fastest executor in the world is just working for someone else's judgment.
